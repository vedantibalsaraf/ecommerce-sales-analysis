BUSINESS INSIGHTS REPORT
E-commerce Sales Data Analysis Using Python
================================================================================
Dataset note: ALL figures below come from a SYNTHETIC e-commerce dataset.
It contains no real customers or transactions. Findings demonstrate the
analysis method and must not be treated as real market evidence.

Scope: 1,500 unique orders | 2,825 units | 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31
Net revenue after discounts: ₹47,11,999.65 (INR)

EXECUTIVE SNAPSHOT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Total net revenue: ₹47,11,999.65
- Unique orders: 1,500
- Units sold: 2,825
- Average order value: ₹3,141.33
- Leading category: Electronics (₹18,45,661.00)
- Strongest month: November 2025 (₹6,53,958.80)
- Top product by revenue: Smartwatch (₹8,01,556.80)
- Leading city: Mumbai (₹6,47,269.95)

FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Electronics is the highest-revenue product category
   What the data shows: Electronics generated ₹18,45,661.00, or 39.17% of net revenue, from 352 orders and 670 units. The next two categories were Home & Kitchen (₹11,09,118.15) and Fashion (₹6,24,122.10).
   Suggested action: Keep Electronics well stocked and test focused campaigns for it. Also check whether its revenue is accompanied by heavy discounting before increasing investment; the data alone does not show profitability.

2. November 2025 was the strongest month for revenue
   What the data shows: November 2025 produced ₹6,53,958.80 (13.88% of annual revenue) from 210 unique orders and 405 units. That is 66.54% above the average month of ₹3,92,666.64.
   Suggested action: Use this peak period for capacity, inventory and staffing planning. The dataset shows timing but cannot prove why the peak occurred, so validate the pattern with real operational data.

3. Smartwatch leads product revenue
   What the data shows: Smartwatch generated ₹8,01,556.80 (17.01% of total) from 163 units. The top three products together contributed ₹16,34,792.75 (34.69%). By units, Smartwatch led with 163 units.
   Suggested action: Protect availability and visibility for the leading products, and review their discount levels rather than assuming high revenue means high profit. High concentration also creates stock-out risk.

4. Mumbai is the leading city by revenue
   What the data shows: Mumbai contributed ₹6,47,269.95 (13.74% of total). The top three named cities were Mumbai (₹6,47,269.95), Delhi (₹6,05,395.75), Bengaluru (₹5,70,880.00), together accounting for 38.70% of revenue. Orders with an unknown city numbered 12 and contributed ₹19,123.90; improving city capture would make geographic reporting more complete.
   Suggested action: Prioritise fulfilment and local campaigns in the strongest cities, while treating the ranking as descriptive. Improve city data capture at checkout so the comparison is complete.

5. Monthly revenue is volatile rather than steadily growing
   What the data shows: Revenue increased in 7 of 11 month-over-month comparisons and decreased in 4. The largest rise was in October 2025 (₹1,69,653.90, +44.95%), while the largest fall was in December 2025 (-₹2,19,052.10, -33.50%).
   Suggested action: Investigate the high-change months by category, product and city before repeating any promotion or stock plan. Do not assume the changes were caused by seasonality; the dataset does not contain marketing spend, competitor or causal data.

6. Average order value and payment mix point to basket opportunities
   What the data shows: The average order value is ₹3,141.33 across 1,500 unique orders. UPI is the largest payment method by revenue, with ₹17,61,241.25 (37.38% of revenue) and 39.40% of orders.
   Suggested action: Test bundles or order-value thresholds to lift the average order value, and monitor checkout reliability for the leading payment method. These are hypotheses to test, not proven revenue drivers.

RECOMMENDATIONS AT A GLANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Plan inventory and campaigns around Electronics, while reviewing discount depth and profitability.
2. Prepare capacity for the November 2025 peak, then validate it on real data before treating it as a recurring seasonal pattern.
3. Keep top-selling products available and reduce concentration risk by developing a second strong product group.
4. Improve city capture at checkout and focus local execution on the highest-revenue cities.
5. Investigate large month-to-month swings with category, product and city breakdowns rather than assuming a cause.
6. Test basket-building offers and monitor the leading payment method.

DATA NOTES AND LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- This is synthetic data, not a real business dataset.
- Revenue is net of discounts and excludes costs, refunds and returns.
- Missing discounts were treated as 0% by the cleaning rule.
- Average order value = total net revenue / unique order_id count.
- Figures describe associations in this dataset; they do not establish causation.
- Only one synthetic year is covered, so long-term seasonality cannot be verified.
